## What it costs

A degree abroad has two big costs. The first is **tuition fees**, paid to the university. The second is **living costs**: rent, food, travel and phone.

## What you pay, and when

You do not pay everything at once. Payments come in this order:

1. **Application fees.** Some universities charge a fee to apply. Many do not.
2. **Deposit to the university.** After an offer, many universities ask for a deposit to hold the seat. This is part of the first year's fees.
3. **Proof of money for the visa.** Before the visa, you must show money in a bank account or a loan letter. The money stays yours. The country page lists the exact amount.
4. **Visa fee and health cover.** Paid on the government's official website.
5. **Flight and accommodation deposit.** Usually one to two months before travel.
6. **Remaining fees and monthly living costs.** After your child arrives.

<Callout>
Pay university fees only to the university's own bank account. Pay visa fees only on the government's official website. Never pay these into a person's account.
</Callout>

## How education loans work

Most families use a loan for part of the cost. There are two main kinds.

**Loan with collateral.** You give property or a fixed deposit as security. The bank can lend a larger amount, and the interest rate is usually lower.

**Loan without collateral.** No property is needed. The bank looks at the university, the course and the co-applicant's income. The amount is usually smaller and the interest is usually higher.

In both cases a parent is usually the co-applicant.

### Repayment

Most education loans give a **moratorium**. This is a period when you do not pay the full instalment, usually the course length plus a few months. Interest still adds up during this time. Paying the interest during the course keeps the final loan smaller.

Use our [loan EMI calculator](/tools/loan-emi-calculator/) to see the monthly payment for your amount.

### If your CIBIL score is low

A low CIBIL score does not always mean no loan. These options can help:

- Add a co-applicant with a better credit history, such as another parent or relative.
- Offer collateral, which reduces the risk for the lender.
- Apply to a lender that specialises in education loans.
- Pay part of the cost from savings, so the loan amount is smaller.

Our counsellors help you compare lenders and prepare the documents. The lender makes the final decision.

## Safety, accommodation and arrival

**Before your child flies,** we help arrange accommodation near the campus. You will know the address before departure.

**Pre-departure briefing.** We explain the flight, money, phone, the first week, and the work rules for the country.

**Things to do as a family:**

- Keep copies of the passport, visa, offer letter and accommodation details at home.
- Save the address and phone number of the nearest Indian embassy or consulate.
- Agree on a regular time to call each week.
- Check that the course includes or requires health insurance, and keep the policy details.

## How to spot a fake agent

Fake agents take money from families every year. Watch for these warning signs:

- **They guarantee a visa.** Only the embassy or immigration office decides. No agent can promise a visa.
- **They offer a "work permit" without a real job offer.** A real work visa needs a real employer.
- **They will not give written fee terms.** Ask for every fee in writing before you pay anything.
- **They ask you to pay university or visa fees into their own account.**
- **They keep your original passport or certificates without a written receipt.**
- **They push you to decide today.**

You can check any offer letter directly with the university by email. You can check visa rules on the government's official website.
